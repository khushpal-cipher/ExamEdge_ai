## Problem Statement
ExamEdge AI works (branded portal per school, AI tutor answering only from the school's own notes, AI study tools,
tests). Many Indian coaching institutes said "we just don't want it", so there is no paying customer. The founder is
moving to US private schools (small Christian/independent schools and microschools, grades 6-12, 100-400 students,
Florida/Texas/Arizona). A 76-item US build plan exists (`docs/US_BUILD_PLAN.md`, 7-9 weeks). The question: what
should happen in the next 3 weeks so the US move does not repeat the India pattern of building first and hearing
"no" later?

## Demand Evidence
None yet. No US school, head or teacher has seen the product, asked for it, or paid. All US direction comes from
desk research.

## Status Quo
Unknown. The founder does not yet know what a US private school uses today when a student is stuck at night or a
teacher needs a quiz from their own notes. Desk research says free tools exist (ChatGPT, MagicSchool, SchoolAI,
Khanmigo, Gemini/Copilot for Education), but whether these schools use them, ban them, or have no policy is unknown.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- Not yet named. Working guess: the **head of school** at a small private (often Christian) school. Accountable to
  tuition-paying parents and a board. Decides alone.
- Candidate wedge (to test, not build): **"your school's own safe AI"**. Branded, answers only from the school's
  approved materials, hints instead of answers, and the head can see what students ask.
- The wedge is chosen after conversations, not before.

## Constraints
- Solo founder in India; US school day is the founder's night.
- No US phone number today; outreach by email, website contact forms, LinkedIn. Most heads' emails are not public.
- Repo is public: contact lists stay in `sales/` (git-ignored).
- The coaching product stays frozen; US work happens in a separate copy (`docs/US_BUILD_PLAN.md`, step 0.2).
- Open must-fix in the live coaching site: payment draft race (F1), to be fixed before any copy is made.

## Premises (agreed)
1. Don't build Phases 1-6 of the US plan until 10 US school conversations are done.
2. The first goal is learning (what schools use today, who is in pain), not selling.
3. **Revised after second opinion:** the competitor is either free AI tools or "no AI at all" (fear of ChatGPT,
   no policy). The first 10 conversations decide which.
4. Start with one wedge someone would pay for, not the full platform.
5. Success by week 3: 10 conversations, 1 named person in pain, 1 school saying it is willing to try it with real
   students (willingness only; a real-student pilot waits for the privacy step, see Dependencies).

## Cross-Model Perspective
Independent Claude subagent (cold read of a summary):
- **Steelman:** for small private schools, generic AI that ignores their faith, values and curriculum is a liability.
  ExamEdge can be "our school's own AI": their brand, only their approved content, and the head can see what
  students ask. The buyer is the head, not teachers chasing free tools.
- **Key insight:** "I can't name one yet" repeats the India pattern: features first, no named person in pain.
- **Challenged premise #3:** the real competitor may be doing nothing. Small Christian schools may ban AI or fear
  it, so the pain is control and visibility, not missing features. Test: in 10 conversations, count "no or
  restricted AI" vs "uses free AI tools". If 6 or more are restricted, the wedge is governance.
- **Prototype suggestion:** a private demo per school built from its public website, a hints-only tutor, a
  head-of-school view, and a 2-minute video. Skip billing, hosting, legal and new features. Get a US VoIP number.
- Caution kept: never show a school's brand publicly without permission.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Talk now, personalise only for heads who reply (chosen)
About 1 day of build, then 30-50 question-led messages. Private school-specific demo only for heads who reply.
### Approach B: Personalised demo for every school first
Ruled out for now: 3-4 days of building before the first conversation, and brand-permission risk. Kept as the
follow-up for interested heads.
### Approach C: Lead with a free "Safe AI policy" call, no product
Ruled out as the main path (risks positioning as a consultant), but its question ("How is your school handling
AI?") is used as the opening in Approach A.

## Recommended Approach
**Approach A.** It starts real conversations this week and tests premise #3 directly.

**Outreach starts on day 1 and is NOT blocked by any code work.** Only the demo kit waits for steps 0.1-0.2.

**Demo kit (build, about 1 day with Claude Code, in the US copy after steps 0.1-0.2 of the US plan):**
1. US words on the screens a head will see (school, class, chapter, question).
2. One clearly fictional demo school ("Maple Grove Demo Academy", Grade 8 Science) seeded with notes, a quiz and
   a test.
3. Tutor "hints only" switch for the demo.
4. A 2-minute screen recording of the demo.
5. Delivery: live screen-share on Zoom/Google Meet from the founder's local copy. No production deploy until the
   week-3 review.

Nothing else from `docs/US_BUILD_PLAN.md` is built until the week-3 review.

**Outreach (founder, weeks 1-3):**
- Volume: **about 150 contacts** over 3 weeks (cold replies are usually 1-5%), Florida first, then Texas and
  Arizona, from `sales/us_private_schools.csv`.
- Channels: email (school inbox or head), website contact form, LinkedIn note, plus warm routes: state private-school
  associations, Christian school networks (ACSI, state associations), microschool networks, and US private-school
  leader groups on LinkedIn/Facebook (ask for 15-minute "learning" calls, not sales).
- Opening question, not a pitch: "How is your school handling students using AI like ChatGPT?"
- In every conversation ask: what students use when stuck at night; whether the school has an AI policy or ban;
  what costs teachers the most time; what would be worth paying for.
- Heads who reply and are interested get a private live demo with their own name and mission text (shown only to
  them; no use of their logo or brand anywhere public).

**Tracking columns** (add to `sales/us_private_schools.csv`): `contacted_on`, `channel`, `replied` (yes/no),
`call_on`, `ai_stance` (bans / no policy / uses free tools / unknown), `pain_in_their_words`, `would_try`
(yes/no/maybe), `next_step`.

## Open Questions
- Do most of these schools restrict AI, have no policy, or already use free tools? (decides the wedge)
- Will heads reply to cold email from an India-based founder, or is a US phone number needed? Decide at the end of
  week 1 (about 50 contacts): if fewer than 2 replies, get a US calling option. Check before paying that the provider
  signs up from India without a US ID (candidates: OpenPhone/Quo, Rebtel/Viber Out for outgoing calls only;
  Google Voice and Skype are not options).
- What would a school pay, and per student or per school?

## Success Criteria
By 2026-11-29 (week 3), from about 150 contacts:
- 10 real conversations with heads of school or senior staff (minimum acceptable: 5).
- At least 1 named person with a pain in their own words.
- At least 1 school saying it would try it with real students.
- `ai_stance` recorded for every conversation.

**One gate, one rule** (this replaces step 0.5's stop rule in `docs/US_BUILD_PLAN.md`): nothing in Phases 1-6 of
the US plan is built until the week-3 review. At the review:

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| Fewer than 5 conversations from ~150 contacts | Stop building. Change the message, channel or segment and run 3 more weeks. |
| 5+ conversations, 6+ of 10 say "bans AI" or "no policy" | Wedge = **governance**: "your school's own safe AI" (hints-only tutor, own content only, head sees questions). Build that first. |
| 5+ conversations, most already use free AI tools | Wedge = whatever pain they name that free tools miss (parent report, own-notes tutor, time saved). Build only that. |
| 1+ school says it would try with real students | Do the privacy step (US privacy policy, terms, age check, US hosting) before any student logs in. |

## Dependencies
